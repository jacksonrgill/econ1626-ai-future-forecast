Prologue — 2024
Thesis

By 2027–2030, AI will bifurcate the accounting and financial advisory industry: compliance-heavy commodity work will be largely automated, concentrating productivity gains in large firms and platforms. The advisory relationship becomes more valuable — but harder to access at the mid-tier. Without active policy, this dynamic reinforces market concentration and locks displaced junior accountants out of the transition.

The wave is already here.

In 2013, Oxford researchers Frey and Osborne assigned accountants and bookkeepers a 94% probability of automation — the highest of any licensed profession. A decade later, the tools arrived. GPT-4-class models can draft tax returns, reconcile accounts, and sample audit populations faster than any junior accountant.

The question is no longer whether AI reshapes accounting. It's how fast, who captures the gains, and who bears the cost.

Government adoption incentives accelerate the automation side. Whether reinstatement follows depends on complementary investment in skills and competition.

Economic mechanism: Autor, Levy & Murnane (2003) showed that automation targets routine cognitive tasks — those sufficiently codifiable to be replicated algorithmically — rather than entire occupations. Bookkeepers and junior accountants spend 70–90% of their time on such tasks. Senior advisors spend the majority of their time on judgment-intensive, non-routine work. Displacement is therefore within the profession, not of it — but the distribution matters enormously.

Chapter 3 — The Concentration Problem
74%

High
Platform switching costs — low data portability traps SMEs once committed to a vendor
Economic mechanism: AI-driven markets exhibit strong data network effects — platforms with more users generate more training data, producing better AI, attracting more users. This creates winner-takes-most dynamics. Acemoglu & Restrepo (2018) argue that firms with large proprietary datasets hold a durable structural advantage that competes on terms no regulatory framework has yet fully addressed. The accounting software market is a textbook case.

Uncertainty & Structural Break Risks
What could make all three scenarios wrong: These projections assume no AGI-level capability jump before 2030 and that the current regulatory pace holds. A step-change in AI reasoning capability — closing the gap on judgment-intensive advisory tasks — would compress the upside/downside range dramatically. Conversely, a major AI liability or privacy incident could slow adoption across the board, shifting all scenarios toward a later horizon.
Data gaps: The most significant gap is granular task-level time-use data for Australian accounting roles. Frey & Osborne (2013) used US occupational definitions; Australian task profiles may differ, particularly for small-practice generalist accountants who perform advisory and compliance work within the same role. The OECD (2023) acknowledges this methodological constraint explicitly.

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Employment projections are illustrative scenario estimates calibrated to published ranges — not point forecasts
  • Market share extrapolates from Australian IBISWorld data; global dynamics may differ materially
  • Assumes no AGI-level capability jump before 2030; structural break risk is not modelled
  • Policy timelines assume standard parliamentary and regulatory processes (~18–24 months for major legislation)
  • Automation probability estimates (Frey & Osborne, 2013) pre-date GPT-4; actual displacement may be significantly faster
